Sometimes, while working with AI, I realize I have spent a long time reviewing and revising without really getting anywhere.

I read a suggestion, accept it if it makes sense, and ask for changes if something is missing. While I check the revision, another suggestion comes up. That seems useful too, so I work it in. None of these steps seems wrong on its own. With all this reading and editing, it feels as though I am getting a fair amount of work done.

But when I think back to what I originally wanted to do, I struggle to put it into words. I came to AI to make sense of a difficult situation or work through a strategic problem. Somewhere along the way, I find myself occupied with reviewing its suggestions.

This is especially easy to fall into when I am not clear about what I want. I asked AI for help because I was struggling to make a judgment on my own. Yet I still have to decide how much of its advice to accept. Without a standard to judge it by, I keep going in the hope that another pass will help. What exactly needs to improve remains vague.

Reviewing carefully does not necessarily mean I am directing the work. I can check for awkward sentences or missing explanations while losing sight of why I am doing the task and how far it needs to go. On a difficult decision, I may even accept the comparison criteria AI proposes without questioning them. All the review that follows then takes place within those criteria.

A study presented at CHI in 2025 surveyed 319 knowledge workers about their use of generative AI. In their responses, the researchers observed a shift in effort from doing the work directly toward checking information, applying AI responses to their work, and managing the direction of the task. This was a survey of people's work experiences, not a measurement of changes in their actual thinking ability.[1]

Applied to my experience, this suggests that I may be so occupied with improving the answer that I neglect to direct the work. I can make what AI produces better and still need to consider whether it is the work I need right now.

It may help to think in terms of input, process, and output: what information and constraints I have, how to work with them, and what I want to get from the task. This could be a useful way to frame a request even if you are not a developer.

I would start with the output. Reviewing a strategy to understand the differences between several options calls for different work than reviewing it to choose one. Deciding how far this particular task should go helps establish which materials are needed and how much more investigation is necessary.

Then I can ask AI to help gather and organize information or compare assumptions. I also need to supply the practical circumstances and constraints I know. Even when I delegate the input and process, I need to know what the work is supposed to achieve. When the result comes back, I check it against that purpose. As well as checking whether the answer is well put together, I need to check whether I can now make the judgment I intended to make.

I cannot set every criterion in advance. Talking with AI may change my thinking, and some problems only become clear after several revisions. If each round narrows the options or makes the next thing to check clearer, the repetition is serving a purpose. If I am only revising the answer over and over, I need to consider how those changes are helping me decide.

When I notice that I am going in circles, it may be worth pausing before accepting the next suggestion. I can try writing one sentence about the decision I am trying to make through this work. If I struggle to write it, I probably need to go back to that question with AI.
