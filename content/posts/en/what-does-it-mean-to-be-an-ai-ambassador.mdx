What would it take for me to become an ambassador for Claude or ChatGPT, beyond knowing how to use AI well?

I see the core role as helping AI reach more people. That starts with using the product myself and finding situations where it is useful. I need to understand it well enough to explain those uses clearly to others.

I first thought about ambassadors for brands like Chanel or Gucci. What interested me was how a person could make a product feel familiar. I wondered whether AI could work in a similar way. Watching someone use it for work or a hobby might make another person want to try it. Creating that interest without explaining every feature seems like a good way to introduce a product.

The approach would depend on whom I was speaking to.

People who already follow AI may research products and experiment on their own. I could introduce a use they had not considered or show them what a particular product does well. Helping them develop a positive impression of the company and its products would also have value.

I am more interested in reaching people who pay little attention to AI. A new feature may mean nothing to them. I could spend a long time explaining something I find exciting, while they have no reason to care about it.

This makes paying attention to people an important part of the role. I would need to understand what they enjoy, what they find tedious, and what they keep wanting to do but putting off. Those conversations could help me identify a useful connection to AI.

Suppose someone is planning a trip. They have several places they want to visit, but putting together an itinerary feels like a chore. I could suggest working through it with AI. The conversation would still be about their trip. AI would enter as something they could use along the way. They might find a reason to try it without first becoming interested in the technology itself.

This is what I have in mind when I think about introducing AI without pressure. Start with something the person already cares about. Help them work on it without making a new technology feel like another subject they have to study.

Up to this point, the role overlaps considerably with sales and marketing. It involves making a product known, building interest, and encouraging people to use it. When I first wrote this note, I thought getting someone with no prior interest in AI to try it would be a good outcome for an ambassador.

That leaves the question of what happens after the first attempt.

In the travel example, the suggested itinerary might not be useful. The person might stop because they do not know what additional information to provide. Checking the results might take more effort than they expected. They could try the product once and decide not to return.

If they found the experience disappointing, explaining the product’s strengths again might not help. I would first need to understand what they expected, what they tried, and where they got stuck. They might need some guidance. The product might lack a necessary feature. I might also have recommended a task that was a poor fit for the tool.

The official ambassador programs describe a related responsibility. Anthropic asks Claude Community Ambassadors to bring their communities’ perspectives to its product teams. OpenAI’s Codex Ambassadors program emphasizes community interests and needs, and provides opportunities to give feedback directly to the OpenAI team.

The role extends from introducing the product to people to bringing their experiences back to the company.

Listening matters on both sides of that process. Before recommending AI, I would want to understand what someone hoped to do. Afterward, I would want to know how it went. The benefit I expected them to find might differ from what they actually valued. A step I thought I had explained clearly might be where they struggled. Their experience could change what I recommend next and how I explain it.

There are limits to this role. An ambassador cannot support every person indefinitely or resolve every problem. I would need to define what I can help with directly and which issues to collect and pass on to the product team. Still, if the role includes understanding the experience after someone starts using AI, getting them to try it once would not be the end of the work.

I began by considering how well I could use AI and how effectively I could present it. I would like to give more attention to what happens for the people I encourage to try it. That requires product knowledge and an interest in people. Sometimes it would mean setting aside what I wanted to show so I could listen to what they had to say.

The kind of ambassador I have in mind is someone people can continue talking to after the recommendation. They should feel comfortable saying that the product helped, that they are still confused, or that it was not useful. If I became an ambassador, I would want to start by building relationships where those conversations could happen.
